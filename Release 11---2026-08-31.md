## SorghumBase Release 11
### Released: September 2026

SorghumBase (https://www.sorghumbase.org) is a USDA-ARS funded resource released to the public in 2021. The SorghumBase team works closely with the community to support stewardship of sorghum genomics data, establishes best-practices on managing the data, and provides opportunities for networking and capacity building within the community. We maintain a database of scientific publications, genome sequences, gene structure annotations, and comparative genomic analyses integrated with curated data from [EBI's Gene Expression Atlas](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gxa/plant/experiments), [Bio-Analytical Resources' eFPseq browser](https://bar.utoronto.ca/), [Gramene's Plant Reactome](https://plantreactome.gramene.org), and [OZ Sorghum QTL Atlas](https://aussorgm.org.au/sorghum-qtl-atlas/).


## Summary

In its eleventh release, SorghumBase (SB) features 2 new _Sorghum bicolor_ ssp. _bicolor_ genomes, 7 existing genomes with updated gene sets, all selected from the community prioritized list. The site now hosts a total of 120 sorghum genome assemblies. Protein-coding gene family analysis constructed 50,826 gene trees from 2,673,286 input gene models representing 71 sorghum and 8 outgroup genomes. Standard identifiers for genetic variation (rsIDs) assigned by the European Variation Archive (EVA) to variants called on BTx623 v3 have been projected to 100 other sorghum assemblies. This resource allows traits to be anchored to stable identifiers and accessed in a pan-genome context.

In an effort to harmonize germplasm identifiers within SorghumBase and based on the recommendation of best practices used by the community and curators of major sorghum germplasm repositories such as GRIN and ICRISAT, where available, we provide genotyping data using standard germplasm identifiers (e.g., PI and IS numbers) and standard SNP identifiers (e.g., rsIDs). 

In the Germplasm tab of the gene search page, accessions with genetic variants causing the Loss of Functions (LOFs) are now organized by study or population, type of variant (e.g., stop gained/lost, frameshift, splice acceptor/donor) and homozygous/heterozygous state.

Besides genomic resources, SorghumBase provides weekly updates of news items, conferences, community events and funded research projects. Our publications database now includes over 1,800 sorghum papers, a subset of which have been [highlighted in News posts](https://www.sorghumbase.org/posts?categories=research-highlights). News items and project pages are indexed and searchable, and include information on how to access related data on SorghumBase. A recently updated [user guide](https://www.sorghumbase.org/guides) is available from the site and feedback may be provided via a [contact form](https://www.sorghumbase.org/contact). Individual training sessions may be scheduled upon request. Please check the upcoming [events](https://www.sorghumbase.org/events) on the latest webinars, conferences, meetings, etc. relevant to the sorghum research community. You are always welcome to [contact us](https://www.sorghumbase.org/contact), if you are interested in hosting a training event, interested in collaboration with the SorghumBase team or would like to provide feedback or ask questions. 

Funding for the project is provided by the Agricultural Research Service of the [U.S. Department of Agriculture](http://www.usda.gov/) (USDA ARS 8062-21000-051-00D). 


## What's New in release 11?

### New Data

***Genomes & Gene Annotations***

Genomes & Gene Annotations

Release 11 expands the Sorghum bicolor genomic resources in SorghumBase with three new genomes—PI 533794, PI 533831, and PI 533991—and updated annotations for six genomes: PI 552861, PI 561072, PI 561073, PI 655993, PI 656029, and PI 656068.

The three new genomes were community-prioritized selections nominated by the Sorghum Community Working Group. PI 533794 (SC110) is an Ethiopian-derived sorghum conversion line; PI 533831 (IS 2508C) is a Sudanese-derived line reported as a source of sorghum downy mildew resistance; and PI 533991 (SC748/IS 3552C) is a Sudanese-derived line and an important source of anthracnose resistance used in U.S. sorghum breeding.

Annotations for all nine genomes were generated or updated using PacBio Kinnex full-length mRNA sequencing, providing direct evidence for transcript structures, exon–intron boundaries, and alternative splice isoforms. These data improve the representation of gene structure and isoform diversity across sorghum genomes, providing richer accession-specific annotations for comparative and pan-genome analyses.

### Gene Search: New Features and Enhancements

The SorghumBase gene search interface has been substantially redesigned and expanded for Release 11, providing more powerful tools for exploring gene relationships, expression, functional annotations and comparative genomics. The updated interface also introduces improved filtering, visualization, data export and sharing capabilities. 

#### Redesigned Homology and  Gene-tree Viewer

The Homology tab for each gene now features [TBrowse](https://tbrowse.org), a new interactive viewer that integrates the phylogenetic gene tree with multiple aligned data tracks in a single pannable, zoomable and fullscreen-capable interface. 

Key features include: 

- **Gene Tree** — Collapse or expand clades, prune and regrow lineages, pivot the tree around a focal gene, and search for and highlight specific genes. The tree is automatically pruned according to the genomes selected by the user. 
- **Multiple sequence alignment (MSA)** — Smooth pan and zoom navigation with a minimap, multiple residue-coloring schemes including Clustal and hydrophobicity, protein-domain annotations, splice-junction marks and live residue nformation on hover.  Collapsed-clades can also display consensus sequences, with optional gap masking for more compact visualization. 
- **Genomic neighborhood** — Explore conserved gene-neighborhood (synteny) context with genes colored by gene family. Gene lengths are now represented proportionally, while long inergenic regions are compressed to improve readability. 
- **Gene structure** — View exon/intron gene models alongside the phylogenetic tree. 
- **Expression** — Explore summarized and harmonized expression data from EMBL-EBI Expression Atlas directly within the gene tree, enabling comparison of expression patterns across species.
- **Customizable tracks** Tracks can be shown/hidden, reordered, resized, and switched between data tracks. Users can also adjust row height and font size to create either compact or expanded views. By default, one detailed track is displayed at a time, although tracks can be unlinked for simultaneous viewing. 

#### New and Reworked Result Views

Release 11 introduces several new and substantially improved ways to explore gene search results:

- **Taxonomic distribution** - An interactive species tree displays the genome-wide distribution of genes matching the search criteria. Users can pan and zoom, examine gene counts for individual genomes, and drag-select taxonomic regions to use them as filters.  
- **Gene attributes** — A compact gene × attribute heatmap summarizes characteristics across search results including tissue expression, stress responses, and other gene-associated attributes. Columns can be sorted, and individual cells can be selected to filter results. 
- **Expression visualization** — Enhanced heatmaps  provide sticky labels, binned color scales, tree-based field searching, linked table to plot hovering, brushing, log-scale toggle, and TSV export.
- **Ontology enrichment** — Perform species specific ontology enrichment (over-representation analysis) directly from gene search results, supported by a persistent ontology cache and integrated help. 
- **Gene list** Alternate identifiers are now displayed beneath the primary gene ID, making it easier to recognize genes across different annotation systems and resources. 

#### Smarter search and filtering

Gene Search now provides more flexible and intuitive tools for refining and expanding queries. 
- A new **Refine** panel in the sidebar surfaces facet counts, allowing users to narrow search results based on values actually represented in the current result set. 
- Search results can now be filtered by  **genomic interval**.
- Searches can be **Expanded along orthologs/paralogs or to genomic neighbours** in one click.
- Existing filters can be edited directly from their associated menus without rebuilding the search. 

#### Save, share, and export

- **Shareable saved views** — Capture the complete state of a Gene Search session, including filters and individual view configurations, in a sharable  link that restores the same view when opened.
- **User gene lists** — Upload, create, save, and share personal gene lists. Gene lists support trash and restore functionality as well as automatic resolution of identifier conflicts during list creation. Signing in is required to save gene lists. 
- **Data exporter** — Build customized exports using a hierarchical catalog of available fields. Preview columns can be reordered and resized before export, and newly added sorghum_v11 fields are organized into clearly labeled groups.  

#### Platform, Performance, and Mobile Improvements
The Gene Search application has also received substantial underlying platform and usability improvements. 

- Rebuilt using **React 18**  with fullscreen detail tabs and drag-to-resize panels.
- Improved startup performance through streamlined initial queries, removal of redundant requests, and extended caching of stable reference data.  
- Enhanced support for smaller screens through collapsible sidebar sections, responsive tables, and a more compact pathways panel.

### Variation

Standard rsID identifiers assigned by the EVA were mapped to nearly 46 million SNPs in both BTx623 reference assemblies (NCBIv3 and JGIv5.1), as well as ????? other accessions including 46 ex-PVP breeding lines (see Variation column in the [Species Table](https://ensembl.sorghumbase.org/species.html) for a complete list. In addition, rsIDs were also assigned to Arabidopsis, Japonica rice and B73 maize SNPs.


**BTx623 (NCBIv3)**

Over 78 million sorghum variants including more than 65 million naturally occurring SNPs genotyped in over 4,450 accessions, and almost 13 million chemically-induced variants (i.e., ethyl methanesulfonate (EMS)-induced mutations) in a mutagenized population BTx623 background. This data was sourced from the following projects:

***Natural variation***

* [Sorghum Genomics Toolbox](https://www.globalsorghuminitiative.org/) - 36.1M SNPs, 940 resequenced genomes
* [Kumar et al (2024)](https://www.sorghumbase.org/paper/24501) - 18.7M SNPs and indels, whole-genome sequencing 365 accessions (including 332 BAP)
* [Lasky et al (2015)](https://www.sorghumbase.org/paper/24502) - 400K SNPs and indels, 1943 georeferenced landraces
* [Boatwright et al (2022)](https://www.sorghumbase.org/paper/20741) - 33M SNPs and 5M indels genotyped by sequencing 400 SAP lines
* [Lozano et al (2021)](https://www.sorghumbase.org/paper/comparative-evolutionary-genetics-of-deleterious-load-in-sorghum-and-maize) - nearly 13M SNPs, 499 sorghum accessions from the TERRA-MEPP, TERRA-REF population panels, and lines previously genotyped by [Mace et al (2013)](https://www.sorghumbase.org/paper/21275)

***Chemically-induced mutagenesis screens***

* [Jiao et al (2023)](https://www.sorghumbase.org/paper/23165) - 8.6 million EMS-induced mutations, 890 lines
* [Addo-Quaye et al (2018)](https://www.sorghumbase.org/paper/19942) - 2.5 million EMS-indiced mutations, 486 M3 lines
* [Jiao et al (2016)](https://www.sorghumbase.org/paper/a-sorghum-mutant-resource-as-an-efficient-platform-for-gene-discovery-in-grasses) - 1.7 million EMS-induced mutations, 252 M4 mutant pools


***BTx623 v3 rsIDs remapped to other sorghum accessions***

Nearly 46 million rsIDs mapped from EVA release 5 (July 2023) to the genetic variants in sorghum BTx623 NCBIv3 have been remapped to BTx623 JGIv5, 46 ex-PVP sorghum breeding accessions, and 53 additional sorghum genomes in SorghumBase (including 30 newly released in the current release 10).

In the near future, we aim to have rsIDs assigned to all naturally occuring variants in BTx623 in SorghumBase.

### Expression

There are updates on the gene expression data in the curent release. Now the SorghumBase has total 16 bulk expression studies, consisting of 11 studies with baseline expression and 5 with differential expression gene expression data for the _S. bicolor_ BTx623 (NCBIv3) genome reference curated and processed through the [EMBL-EBI Expression Atlas](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gxa/plant/experiments). The Paralogs tab allows the users to select the baseline or differential study to provide the expression of paralogs for the selected reference study and generates the heatmap. These are available through our Gene Search (Expression panel).  
Data for the eFP browser on the Expression tab of the SorghumBase search results have also been updated form one studies to 10 expression studies. It provides maps based on tissue, developmental stages, and stress conditions. 


### Pathways

The Plant Reactome was updated in April, 2025 and includes 9 new pathways nested inside 3 new categories, including "Flowering under drought stress", "Root Gravitropism", and "Shoot Gravitropism". One new pathway "TF network involved in salinity response" was nested under "Response to salinity". In addition,  5 pathways were updated. For more details see the [release summary](https://plantreactome.gramene.org/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=113&Itemid=360&lang=en). This release incorporates the latest update from Plant Reactome in which 351 manually curated pathways involving 1,590 Oryza sativa japonica genes were projected via orthology to Sorghum bicolor BTx623 v3 and the anchor genomes shared with among the Gramene sites.
